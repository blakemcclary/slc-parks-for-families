Why This Organization Exists

Salt Lake City is at an inflection point for families

There are fewer children living in Salt Lake City today than at any point in recent history. In 2024, the city closed four elementary schools due to declining enrollment. Families are quietly voting with their feet, and the urban core is losing the conditions that make it viable to raise children long-term.

"Two things sit at the center of that equation: parks and housing."

For families deciding whether to stay in the city, parks are not a luxury. They are critical infrastructure for daily play, social connection, and community life. And housing is the foundation beneath all of it. If a family cannot find a home they can afford to buy and actually raise children in, no park or amenity will keep them.

Salt Lake City is growing. But it is not growing for families. That has to change.

Parks

The fundamentals
families depend on

Too many of Salt Lake City's parks are failing the families who need them. We focus on practical, achievable changes that improve day-to-day usability — and we hold the city accountable for delivering them.

01

Consistent maintenance

Consistently maintained parks in every neighborhood — trash picked up, equipment repaired, graffiti removed. Not once, but reliably, so families can count on it.

02

Clean, reliable restrooms

Clean and reliable restrooms are not optional for families with young children. A locked or filthy bathroom determines whether you stay for an hour or leave in ten minutes.

03

Safe play environments

Safe play environments free from hazards and unlawful activity — spaces where children can move freely and parents aren't on edge.

04

Accountable public spending

Accountable use of public funds. We publish clear assessments of park conditions, track where maintenance budgets go, and advocate for transparent city spending.

05

Amenities for real family use

Splash pads, shade structures, and modern playgrounds. In a high-desert city with 300 days of sun, these aren't extras — they're what makes parks usable and worth coming back to.

Housing

Homes families
can own

Salt Lake City's housing conversation has largely been shaped around density, affordability, and rental supply. Those are real needs. But the family perspective is often missing from the table. Families need something specific: homes they can own, with space to live in.

🏛

Multi-bedroom for-sale housing as a planning priority

Townhomes and multi-bedroom for-sale product should be an explicit priority in city planning and zoning decisions — not an afterthought. A family with two or three kids cannot put down roots in a 650-square-foot apartment.

📑

Zoning reform for family-scale density

We support zoning reform that enables row houses, cottage clusters, and townhome developments in walkable neighborhoods — smart density that includes families, not density that inadvertently pushes them out.

🔑

Homeownership pathways

Without the ability to purchase rather than rent, families cannot build the stability and equity that ties them to a place long-term. We advocate for policies that make homeownership achievable for families in the city.

🚫

Protecting family-sized housing stock

We oppose policies that crowd out owner-occupied, family-sized housing in favor of investor-owned or short-term rental conversions. The city should be building toward permanence, not transience.

Salt Lake City should be a place where a family can find a home to buy, with room for their kids, in a neighborhood with good parks and good schools. Right now, that is harder than it should be.

Our Approach

Nonpartisan.
Practical.
Pro-family.

We are not anti-growth or anti-development. We believe Salt Lake City can grow and remain a city for families — but only if families are treated as a constituency worth designing for.

"We support smart density that includes family-scale product, not density that inadvertently pushes families out."

On public safety, we support humane, lawful, and effective approaches that pair accountability with appropriate social services — preserving parks and public spaces as places for recreation, play, and community life.

⚖️

Nonpartisan by design

Safe parks and family-scale housing are not left or right issues. We welcome anyone who believes cities need children and public priorities should reflect that.

🏛

Pro-growth, pro-family

We support density — the kind that includes families. Row houses, townhomes, cottage clusters in walkable neighborhoods. Growth that builds the city, not hollows it out.

🤝

Humane and effective on public safety

We support approaches that pair accountability with real social services. Parks and public spaces should serve everyone — especially kids.

📋

Evidence-based advocacy

We publish clear assessments, elevate best practices from peer cities, and show up at city council meetings. We build credibility slowly, on purpose.

Why Now

Without intentional action,
the trend will continue

Salt Lake City is growing, but its family population is shrinking. Parks and housing are the two most immediate levers the city has to reverse course — and both require advocates who are specifically focused on what families need.

Once a city loses its critical mass of families, the dynamics are hard to reverse. Schools close. Family-serving businesses leave. The culture shifts. The time to act is before the tipping point, not after.

A city that grows without families loses something essential.

Cities with strong family populations have higher civic participation, more stable neighborhoods, better schools, and more long-term investment in public infrastructure. Families aren't just residents — they're a city's stake in its own future.

Better parks and family-scale housing are achievable. They don't require waiting for a new mayor or a budget windfall. They require advocates at the table, consistently, making the case that families are a constituency worth designing for.
